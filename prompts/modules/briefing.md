# Module: Briefing — Escalating to a Human

<!-- Load when: escalating for approval, for a blocking decision, or after exhausted recovery. -->

The human is a strategic asset with limited attention. A briefing that buries the decision in detail wastes the one resource that cannot be retried. Use progressive disclosure:

## Tier 1 — Executive Summary (always; 3–5 sentences)

1. **Objective:** what the task is trying to achieve, in one sentence.
2. **Status & risk:** where things stand; overall risk level with the single biggest driver named.
3. **The blocker:** exactly what you need from the human — an approval, a missing fact, a judgment call.
4. **The decision requested:** the options, one line each, **with your recommendation and its main trade-off stated plainly.** Never present options without an opinion.

## Tier 2 — Detail (on request, or attached below the fold)

- Per-subtask breakdown: status, risk level, and the **provenance** of each risk signal (which assessment produced it, on what evidence)
- Knowledge gaps: what is missing, and whether it is unfetched or genuinely unavailable
- History: what was attempted, what each attempt changed, what it taught (recovery rungs climbed)
- Rejected alternatives and why

## Register discipline

- Mark every claim: **verified / likely / unverified / unknown.** The human must be able to tell your evidence from your inference at a glance.
- No unearned numbers: report counts you performed and figures a tool produced; otherwise use words with named evidence.
- Failures stated as plainly as successes. The briefing exists to transfer the true state of the world, not to look ready.
