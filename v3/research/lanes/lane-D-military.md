# Lane D: Military planning, orders and command doctrine

**Method and a limit the reader must know.** Searches ran normally, but the shared web-fetch budget (100 per hour across all agents) ran out partway through. I read in full only a handful of pages. Several doctrine manuals I opened were truncated before the chapters I needed (the FM 5-0 copy I reached is the 2005 edition, not the 2022 one). Most doctrinal definitions below are therefore tagged `[recalled]` (from training memory, not re-checked), and only a few `[verified]`. Where I give a paragraph number, it is one that a fetched page showed me. Treat every `[recalled]` paragraph or figure as unchecked. The design team should spot-check the doctrine definitions they rely on against FM 5-0 (16 May 2022) and ADP 6-0 (31 July 2019) before quoting them.

## Summary

1. **JORDAN's BACKBRIEF phase is mislabeled.** In doctrine a backbrief is a subordinate telling the commander how they will do their job, after receiving the order. A *confirmation brief* is the earlier, shorter check that the subordinate understood the intent and their task. JORDAN's "BACKBRIEF" (DAG cycle detection, coupling analysis) is a plan-quality check by the planner, which doctrine does not call a backbrief. V3 should add the real thing: workers restate intent and their task in their own words before they start.
2. **Doctrine treats planning as parallel and time-boxed; JORDAN's pipeline is serial.** The one-third / two-thirds rule and warning orders exist so that subordinates start work on partial information. JORDAN has no analogue (a worker dispatched early with a WARNO-style brief while the lead finishes the plan).
3. **The assumptions rule is the most transferable and most under-used item.** Assumptions must be logical, realistic and essential; each must be validated or converted to a risk with a trigger. JORDAN has a "known / cached / fetchable / genuine gap" research split but no assumption register with trigger conditions.
4. **Decision support is missing from JORDAN.** Doctrine ties CCIR (priority intelligence requirements and friendly force information requirements) to decision points, named areas of interest and indicators, so a commander is only interrupted for a pre-identified decision. JORDAN's premortem watchdogs are close, but they are not tied to named decisions and branches.
5. **The execution-decision versus adjustment-decision split, and the Rapid Decision-making and Synchronization Process (RDSP), answer JORDAN's REPLAN weakness.** JORDAN's REPLAN is one ladder. Doctrine separates "run a pre-planned branch at its trigger" (cheap, no new planning) from "adjust to an unanticipated variance" (RDSP: one satisfactory course of action, not three).
6. **Mission command is a seven-part system, not "send intent".** Competence, mutual trust, shared understanding, intent, mission orders, disciplined initiative, risk acceptance. JORDAN encodes intent and mission orders only. The missing parts translate directly: competence = per-model capability qualification; mutual trust = measured track record; risk acceptance = pre-declared authority limits.
7. **Doctrinal COA practice is known to fail in a specific way** (one real COA, two straw men, per Brender 2025). JORDAN's DEEP tier mandates three COAs and so inherits this failure unless it requires genuinely different strategy vectors and uses the losers as branch plans.
8. **Boyd's OODA is not a loop.** Orientation is the center, with implicit guidance and control bypassing explicit decision. The transferable idea for agents is "shared orientation reduces the need for orders", which argues for investing in shared context files over per-task instructions.
9. **The AAR and lessons-learned machinery is better specified than JORDAN's "experience store".** Four fixed questions, a blame-free rule, and a separate step that turns an observation into a changed doctrine, SOP or rehearsal. Lessons are "observed" but not "learned" unless someone owns the change.
10. **2026 AI-and-C2 writing converges on one claim:** treat agents as subordinates under commander's intent with certification, authority limits and audit logs (Nichols, War on the Rocks, 31 Aug 2026; Annett and Giordano, Breaking Defense, 1 May 2026). It is opinion or concept work, not measured evidence.

## Findings

### 1. MDMP: the seven steps with inputs and outputs
**What it is.** The Military Decision-Making Process is the Army's staff planning method, with seven steps: (1) receipt of mission, (2) mission analysis, (3) COA development, (4) COA analysis (wargaming), (5) COA comparison, (6) COA approval, (7) orders production, dissemination and transition. Per a 2021 SAMS monograph summarizing FM 6-0 [verified, AD1161084]: step 1 takes time available and first staff estimates and gives initial planning guidance; step 2 includes IPB and produces constraints and the evaluation criteria used later; step 3 produces COA sketches and statements (a COA is "a broad potential solution to an identified problem"); step 4 produces refined COAs, a synchronization matrix and decision support templates per COA; step 5 uses the step 2 criteria and typically a decision support matrix; step 6 yields the commander's decision, final planning guidance, new CCIR and refined intent; step 7 turns the COA statement and sketch into the concept of operations and overlay. The same monograph counts about 60 sub-steps and 49 products in total (author's estimate, not doctrine). At the end of steps 2 to 6 there are formal briefs.
**Source.** Hughes, SAMS monograph 2021, https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/trecms/pdf/AD1161084.pdf [verified]. FM 5-0, Planning and Orders Production, 16 May 2022 is the current authority; it absorbed planning content from FM 6-0 and the Army Design Methodology and assessment ATPs (https://www.army.mil/article/256079) [verified]. The 2005 FM 5-0 [verified, elon.edu copy] sets the one-third/two-thirds rule at para 1-102 and the three minimum WARNOs at para 1-93.
**Critiques.** The CTC (Combat Training Center) trends are that staffs finish MDMP with incomplete operational frameworks and skip an assessment framework, so they brief data instead of analysis (FY23 mission command key observations, https://www.army.mil/article/274300/ [verified]). The Center for Army Lessons Learned published a dedicated MDMP handbook (23-07, Nov 2023) specifically to reverse negative CTC trends, which is itself evidence that the process is not executed as written [verified, https://www.army.mil/article/271773/]. Griffiths (MWI, Oct 2026) says MDMP is laborious, imperfect and produces stale plans, and counts doctrinal attention to RDSP at 7 pages against 56+ for MDMP in FM 5-0 [verified, https://mwi.westpoint.edu/fixing-the-armys-mdmp-fixation/].
**Transfer.** The *artifacts*, not the steps, carry over: a restated mission, a constraints list, evaluation criteria fixed before comparing COAs, a synchronization matrix, a decision support matrix. The time-and-staff rationale does not carry over: agents have no staff-hour scarcity inside a session, but they do have context and credit scarcity, and they cannot be trusted to run steps they merely narrate.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should define each PLAN output as a named artifact with a schema (restated mission, constraints, evaluation criteria, decision points) and check for artifact presence in harness code, not in prose, because the CTC evidence shows that staffs follow the step names while skipping the outputs. Harness code (a schema validator on the plan file).

### 2. Mission analysis sub-steps, facts and assumptions, restated mission, initial CCIR
**What it is.** Mission analysis in ATTP 5-0.1 (2011) has 17 sub-steps [recalled; Figure 4-2 exists, p. 4-7, content not visible in my fetch]. The ones that matter here: analyze the higher headquarters' order (including intent two levels up); perform IPB; determine **specified tasks** (stated in the order), **implied tasks** (must be done to accomplish a specified task, found by analysis) and **essential tasks** (those that must be done to accomplish the mission); review available assets; determine **constraints** (things you must do or must not do, imposed from above); identify **critical facts and assumptions**; perform risk assessment; determine initial CCIR and EEFI; determine the initial ISR plan; update the plan for use of time; develop the **restated mission** (who, what, when, where, why, the five Ws, usually a task and purpose); present the mission analysis brief. [recalled] The doctrinal rule on assumptions: an assumption is a supposition about the current situation or a future event, taken as true in the absence of proof, and it must be logical, realistic and essential to planning; unresolved assumptions are tracked and either validated by information collection or carried as risks. [recalled]
**Source.** ATTP 5-0.1 table of contents https://irp.fas.org/doddir/army/attp5-0-1.pdf [verified only that the figure exists]; definitions from FM 5-0 / ADP 5-0 [recalled].
**Critiques.** Staffs list "implied tasks" mechanically without finding any; assumptions are often stated and then forgotten. Brender (2025) argues that risk management is treated as an afterthought [verified, https://www.army.mil/article/286860/risk_decision_methodology].
**Transfer.** This is the most directly portable part of MDMP. An agent that restates the mission and lists implied tasks before editing code is doing something that measurably matters in practice (hidden requirement discovery), though I found no benchmark for it. Facts versus assumptions maps exactly onto "things I read in the repo this session" versus "things I believe about the codebase".
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should require a three-column ledger at PLAN time (specified / implied / essential tasks) plus an assumption register where each assumption carries a validation command or becomes a named risk with a trigger, because JORDAN v2 has no mechanism that forces an assumption to be checked or demoted to a risk. Kernel (a few lines), enforced by a hook that blocks "done" while any assumption is unvalidated and un-risked.

### 3. The time rule, warning orders and parallel planning
**What it is.** The commander uses about one-third of the available time and gives subordinates two-thirds; parallel planning and collaborative tools can shift this toward one-fifth / four-fifths (2005 FM 5-0 para 1-102 [verified]). The MDMP prescribes a minimum of three warning orders (WARNOs): after receipt of mission, after mission analysis, and after COA approval (para 1-93 [verified]); more may be issued. A WARNO contents list (situation, mission, tentative task organization, earliest movement time, special instructions, time lines, initial CCIR and guidance) is in the manual's annotated format, Figure 12-4 in ATTP 5-0.1 [verified that figure exists; contents recalled].
**Critiques.** WARNO discipline degrades under pressure: CTC observers report orders published without confirmation of receipt (FY23 observations [verified]).
**Transfer.** Agents differ: worker dispatch is cheap, but starting workers on an unstable plan wastes credits. What carries over is the *separation of early information from final orders*. Read-only reconnaissance workers can start from a WARNO-like brief (mission, constraints, what to find) while the lead plans.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should define a "WARNO brief" (mission, constraints, expected output, no write access) that the lead can dispatch to reconnaissance and research workers before the plan is approved, because it lets research overlap planning without letting unapproved work write code. Orchestration layer.

### 4. Abbreviated MDMP, troop leading procedures, and the Army Design Methodology
**What it is.** (a) *Abbreviated/time-constrained MDMP*: the commander directs which steps to compress; the products are the same but produced faster, typically with a single COA. [recalled] (b) *Troop leading procedures* (TLP), for units without a staff, eight steps: receive the mission; issue a WARNO; make a tentative plan; initiate movement; conduct reconnaissance; complete the plan; issue the order; supervise and refine (rehearse, inspect). [recalled] (c) *Army Design Methodology* (ADM) for ill-structured problems: three framing activities, **environment** (current and desired state), **problem** (the tension between them) and **operational approach** (broad actions), with continuous reframing when the environment or understanding changes; its output is a design concept feeding planning guidance. Source material now sits in FM 5-0 (2022) [verified that it was moved there, https://www.army.mil/article/256079; mechanism recalled].
**Critiques.** ADM has been called vague and hard to teach; I did not locate an evaluation with evidence. Smaller militaries have discussed adopting it (Strategy Bridge 2018, link in search results, unread).
**Transfer.** TLP is the closest doctrinal match to a single agent session: one leader, no staff, with "conduct reconnaissance" placed *before* "complete the plan". That ordering (look before finalizing) is already JORDAN's research phase but TLP also puts "supervise and refine" last as an active step, which JORDAN lacks for dispatched workers. ADM's "reframing" is a doctrinal trigger for abandoning a plan premise, which maps to JORDAN's "plan abandonment" rule.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should treat the STANDARD tier as TLP (eight named steps, one lead, no ceremony) and reserve MDMP-shaped artifacts for DEEP, because TLP was designed for exactly the no-staff case and JORDAN currently applies a 12-phase staff pipeline to it. Kernel and orchestration.

### 5. Commander's intent, task and purpose, main effort, nesting
**What it is.** ADP 6-0 (2019) para 1-45: "a clear and concise expression of the purpose of the operation and the desired military end state" [verified]. Earlier editions and my recollection of FM 5-0 give the components as expanded purpose (why, beyond the task), key tasks (what must be done, even if the plan collapses) and end state (the desired condition of the force and terrain) [recalled; confirm against 2022 FM 5-0]. The joint definition adds that intent "helps subordinate and supporting commanders act to achieve the commander's desired results without further orders" [recalled]. A mission statement has the five Ws and is expressed as task and purpose (what the unit does, so that...). The **main effort** is the designated subordinate unit whose mission is decisive; supporting efforts are resourced to enable it. Subordinates are expected to understand intent two levels up [recalled]. Marine doctrine frames intent as purpose, method, end state and ties it to focus of effort (MCDP 1, MCDP 6) [recalled].
**Source.** ADP 6-0, 31 July 2019, https://irp.fas.org/doddir/army/adp6_0.pdf [verified for para numbers listed in finding 6].
**Critiques.** Intent is frequently a restatement of the concept of operations. Fawley (April 2025) warns that commanders waste the visualization at the combined arms rehearsal on motivation instead of translating intent into an operational overview [verified, https://www.army.mil/article/284542].
**Transfer.** Directly portable, and JORDAN's INTENT.md is a good start. Two parts of doctrine JORDAN does not capture: (a) *key tasks* are the things that must still be achieved if the plan fails, which is the test for "what a worker may never trade away"; (b) *two-up nesting* means a worker should receive its own task and the lead's purpose, and ideally the operator's goal, so that when the brief runs out the worker can choose consistently.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should give every worker brief a fixed three-line intent block (purpose, key tasks that survive plan failure, end state) copied from the operator's INTENT.md, plus its own task and purpose, because workers acting on a task statement alone cannot make the "disciplined initiative" decisions that a purpose enables. Orchestration layer template, with a check in the refuter pass that the worker's report speaks to purpose, not only to the task.

### 6. The seven principles of mission command and disciplined initiative
**What it is.** ADP 6-0 (2019) para 1-26 lists seven principles: competence (1-27), mutual trust (1-30), shared understanding (1-36), commander's intent (1-45), mission orders (1-53), disciplined initiative (1-59), risk acceptance (1-66 and 1-69) [all verified]. Mission command is "the Army's approach to command and control" empowering subordinate decision-making and decentralized execution [verified]. Mission orders "emphasize to subordinates the results to be attained, not how" [verified]. Disciplined initiative is the duty to act within intent when the plan no longer fits [verified]. Risk acceptance is distinguished from gambling: reasoned estimation with a reasonable level of information (1-69) [verified]. Earlier editions listed six principles without "competence" [recalled].
**Critiques.** Auftragstaktik's history: Prussian reforms after 1806, Moltke's practice; modern armies repeatedly adopt the language without the culture. Shamir's *Transforming Command* compares US, British and Israeli armies [source title seen in search; content not read]. NDU Press published "Beyond Auftragstaktik: The Case Against Hyper-Decentralized Command" (Feb 2020) [title seen, https://ndupress.ndu.edu/Media/News/News-Article-View/Article/2076032/, not read]; MWI's "Can Mission Command Actually Work?" and Vandergriff's "mission command without educated officers is just foolish" argue that the education and trust precondition is the usual failure [titles seen, not read]. Stephen Bungay's *The Art of Action* (three gaps: knowledge, alignment, effects) is the standard business-side reading [recalled].
**Transfer.** The principles are preconditions, and they are testable for agents: competence (qualification per model and task), trust (track record on prior briefs), shared understanding (the agent can restate intent), risk acceptance (explicit authority limits). Nichols (2026) reaches the same mapping from the AI side, including mission-specific qualification rather than general benchmarks [verified, finding 16].
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should state mission command as a delegation contract with three explicit fields per dispatch (results sought, boundaries the worker may not cross, authority the worker holds to deviate) because "mission orders" without a stated deviation authority leaves the worker to guess whether to stop or improvise. Orchestration layer brief template, with the eval suite measuring whether workers stopped or improvised correctly on seeded surprises.

### 7. The five-paragraph order, FRAGO, WARNO, annexes and the order-type distinctions
**What it is.** The OPORD has five paragraphs: 1 Situation (area of interest, enemy forces, friendly forces, civil considerations, attachments and detachments, assumptions); 2 Mission (the restated mission); 3 Execution (commander's intent, concept of operations, scheme of movement and maneuver, scheme of fires and the rest, tasks to maneuver units, tasks to other units, coordinating instructions: timeline, CCIR, EEFI, risk reduction control measures, rules of engagement); 4 Sustainment; 5 Command and Signal (command, control, signal, with PACE). [recalled; figures 12-1 and 12-2 in ATTP 5-0.1 exist, verified]. A **FRAGO** is an abbreviated order issued after the OPORD to change or execute a branch or sequel; it follows the same five paragraph order but may omit unchanged paragraphs, and states what is unchanged so it can be read alone (2005 FM 5-0 para 1-19 confirms the decision-to-FRAGO link but gives no formal definition [verified]; the rest is recalled). **Annexes** hold specialist detail (intelligence, fires, sustainment, assessment is Annex M [verified in FY23 article]). *Mission orders* versus *detailed command*: mission orders give purpose and constraints; detailed command prescribes method. Tasks can be conditional: "on order" tasks are expected to execute when a trigger or order arrives; "be prepared to" tasks are contingencies with no certain execution; both are sequel/branch machinery. [recalled] A **no-later-than** time bounds when a task must be complete.
**Critiques.** Orders are often published without confirmation of receipt, and the concept of operation often lacks transition criteria and decision points per phase; units should publish delegation-of-authority matrices to keep decentralized execution inside intent (FY23 CTC observations [verified]). Orders also inflate: staff products crowd out the intent.
**Transfer.** The five-paragraph order is a good brief schema (situation, mission, execution with intent, resources, command and communications). JORDAN's pointer briefs with design rulings, fences and acceptance commands already approximate it. Missing: **coordinating instructions** (what workers must coordinate with each other, deadlines, what to report and when) and **command and signal** (who to ask, what to do if the lead is unreachable: the PACE plan). "On order" and "be prepared to" are a clean language for branch triggers.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should adopt SMEAC-like five-part worker briefs with explicit "command and signal" (escalation channel, fallback if the lead is unresponsive, report format and deadline), and a delegation-of-authority matrix per run, because CTC evidence shows the failures are in acknowledgment, timing and authority, not in the situation paragraph. Orchestration layer, with a harness-level acknowledgment (worker must echo mission and fences before editing).

### 8. Confirmation brief versus backbrief, rehearsals, ROC drills
**What it is.** A **confirmation brief** is given by the subordinate to the commander right after receiving the order, to show they understand intent, their own task and how it fits; a **backbrief** comes later during preparation, when the subordinate briefs how they will accomplish the mission, so the commander can find gaps. [recalled; the distinction is not stated in the excerpts I could fetch, and the 2021 SAMS monograph does not mention it.] Rehearsal types in ATTP 5-0.1 Chapter 8 (pp. 8-1 to 8-11): backbrief, combined arms rehearsal, support rehearsal, battle drill or SOP rehearsal [chapter existence verified; the four types recalled]. Techniques (Figure 8-1): full-dress, reduced-force, terrain-model, sketch-map, map, network [recalled]. A ROC drill (rehearsal of concept) walks the plan on a terrain model with commanders speaking only when their element acts. Fawley (2025) describes the 11th ACR's combined arms rehearsal: commander's visualization, fighting the plan on the map or model, S2 role-playing the enemy, a fixed sequence (detection, fires, aviation, protection, maneuver, adjudication), and a note taker tracking the decision support tool [verified]. Purposes: synchronize in time, space and purpose, confirm subordinate schemes are nested, and compensate for rushed orders.
**Critiques.** Fawley notes the common failure of turning a rehearsal into a back brief (people recite instead of fight the plan) [verified]. The FY23 CTC article says PACE plans and distributed command architectures should be rehearsed to find friction before execution [verified].
**Transfer.** For agents, the closest equivalent of a combined arms rehearsal is a **dry run**: run the plan against the real repository in a sandbox or against recorded states, with an adversarial refuter playing "the enemy" (the failure modes). Agents can do this cheaply and repeatedly, which humans cannot. The confirmation brief is a cheap harness check: the worker echoes mission, fences and acceptance command, and the lead compares.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should (a) rename the current BACKBRIEF to PLAN CHECK and (b) add a true confirmation brief step, a one-message worker restatement checked against the brief before any write, because it catches misunderstanding at the lowest cost and JORDAN's current BACKBRIEF catches only structural plan defects. Harness (a pre-edit hook that requires a restatement artifact) and orchestration layer.

### 9. Information doctrine: CCIR, PIR, FFIR, EEFI, IPB, NAIs, indicators
**What it is.** CCIR are the information the commander needs for a specific decision, consisting of priority intelligence requirements (PIR, about the enemy and environment) and friendly force information requirements (FFIR); essential elements of friendly information (EEFI) are what must be protected and are a separate list, not part of CCIR [recalled]. Each CCIR should be tied to a decision point. IPB (ATP 2-01.3) has four steps: define the operational environment, describe environmental effects, evaluate the threat, determine threat courses of action [recalled]. **Named areas of interest** (NAIs) are places where observation discriminates which enemy COA is occurring; **indicators** are observable signs that a COA or condition is occurring; reconnaissance *pull* (collection tasked against requirements) versus *push* (information sent unprompted) frames who initiates reporting [recalled].
**Critiques.** FY23 CTC finding: CCIR are often underdeveloped; each should be tied to a specific decision, with indicators and collection methods feeding the assessment plan; staffs brief raw data, not analysis [verified, https://www.army.mil/article/274300/].
**Transfer.** CCIR is an interrupt filter: a short list of facts that, if observed, force the lead to decide. For agents, this is the right design for worker reporting: workers should not stream progress; they should report only (a) answers to named requirements and (b) triggers. IPB's final step, "determine the enemy's COAs", maps to "enumerate how this task will fail" (JORDAN's premortem), but IPB also adds *named places to look* (NAIs): specific files, tests or logs that discriminate between hypotheses.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should give each plan a CCIR list of at most five items, each written as "if X is observed then decision Y", with the worker reports keyed to them, because it replaces free-form progress reporting with decision-relevant reporting and cuts lead context use. Kernel (the format) and harness (workers' report schema with a `ccir_hit` field).

### 10. COA development, screening, wargaming and comparison
**What it is.** COA screening criteria: feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable, complete [recalled; confirmed in Brender's article partially, see below]. Wargaming methods (ATTP 5-0.1 figures 4-6 to 4-11 [verified to exist]): **belt** (the area divided into phase-lined belts across the battlefield, analyzing events in each), **avenue-in-depth** (one avenue of approach at a time), **box** (a critical area or objective studied in detail); each also in a lines-of-effort variant. The wargame is "action, reaction, counteraction"; its outputs include the synchronization matrix and decision support template. Staff typically analyze against the enemy's *most likely* and *most dangerous* COA. COA comparison uses a **decision matrix**: weighted criteria scored per COA. [recalled]
**Critiques.** The known weaknesses of the decision matrix: weights are chosen after the staff already prefers an option, scores are subjective but look numeric, and criteria overlap. Brender (army.mil, 8 July 2025) says staffs produce one directed COA and two throwaways, argues all three should be viable, notes his risk-decision method fixes suitability, feasibility and distinguishability but not acceptability or completeness, and argues the unchosen COAs, being capable of the end state, should become branch plans and deception plans [verified, https://www.army.mil/article/286860/risk_decision_methodology]. Hughes (2021) notes wargaming leans on quantitative assessment and argues for qualitative factors [verified].
**Transfer.** The "one real COA and two straw men" problem is the exact failure of an LLM asked for three options: it generates variations of one idea, then rates the first highest. A fresh-context agent can produce a COA without seeing the others, which humans cannot. The wargame step maps to a refuter that role-plays failure against each COA.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should require each DEEP COA to be written by a separate fresh-context worker given a different strategy vector, with weights and criteria frozen before any COA is scored, and the non-selected COAs filed as the branch plan for the first trigger, because that removes both the straw-man and the post-hoc-weights failure. Orchestration layer (COA dispatch) with the DEEP module.

### 11. Running estimates, risk management, and approval authority
**What it is.** Each staff section keeps a **running estimate**: a continuous assessment of its functional area (facts, assumptions, status, conclusions, recommendations) feeding every MDMP step [recalled; FY23 CTC finding says corps staffs do not consistently update them, verified]. Army risk management (ATP 5-19) has five steps: identify hazards, assess hazards, develop controls and make risk decisions, implement controls, supervise and evaluate [recalled]. Residual risk is the risk left after controls; the authority to accept it rises with its level (extremely high, high, moderate, low), with the highest levels reserved to senior commanders [recalled; I could not retrieve the specific authority table, so the level-to-echelon mapping is unverified]. ADP 6-0 para 1-69 separates risk acceptance from gambling [verified].
**Transfer.** Residual risk and delegated acceptance authority is a better primitive than a single "risk level then approval" gate: it says who may sign for what *after* mitigation. A running estimate per worker is a status file that is updated, not a one-off summary.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should record residual risk after controls (not just initial risk) and bind approval authority to residual level by role (worker, lead, human), because JORDAN's fused risk gate approves on the pre-control risk and so cannot say what a mitigation bought. Harness (risk fields in the plan schema, hook on authority).

### 12. Execution and assessment: operations process, RDSP, variance, and the decision types
**What it is.** The operations process is plan, prepare, execute and assess, with assessment running throughout [recalled, ADP 5-0]. **Measures of performance** (MOP) assess whether friendly tasks were done; **measures of effectiveness** (MOE) assess whether the situation changed toward the desired end state; **indicators** are the observable data behind each [recalled]. A **variance** is the difference between the plan's forecast and reality. An **execution decision** implements a planned action at a decision point; an **adjustment decision** modifies the operation for an unforeseen opportunity or threat [recalled]. The **RDSP** has five steps, two assessment (determine impact, determine options) and three development (develop, refine and validate, implement); it produces one satisfactory COA, not three. Minor variance within intent is handled by revising the plan; substantial variance returns to MDMP [verified via Griffiths, https://mwi.westpoint.edu/fixing-the-armys-mdmp-fixation/; the decision-type definitions recalled]. Related concepts: battle rhythm (recurring meetings), tactical pause, culminating point (where an attacker can no longer sustain operations), transitions, reserve, battle drills and SOPs (pre-decided responses), liaison officers, battle handover, relief in place, shift-change briefs. [recalled]
**Critiques.** Griffiths: doctrine explains what RDSP is but not how to run it. FY23: rigid adherence to the battle rhythm delayed adjustments that the tempo required, and battle damage assessments listed destroyed targets without saying what changed for either side's options [both verified]. Smallwars Journal (July 2026) has an article on structural limits of Army campaign assessment (title seen, unread). MOP/MOE confusion is a long-standing complaint in assessment doctrine and the JCS assessment handbook (link seen, unread).
**Transfer.** MOP versus MOE is the clearest portable lesson for code agents: "tests I wrote pass" and "diff applied" are MOPs; "the user's stated outcome is met" is an MOE. JORDAN's verifier language covers acceptance criteria but does not force the distinction. Battle drills and SOPs equal pre-decided responses (JORDAN's recovery ladder is one). Shift-change brief, battle handover and relief in place equal JORDAN's handoff files at context limits, and doctrine's required contents (current state, pending decisions, open risks, who owns what) give a checklist.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should split EVALUATE into MOP (did the planned action finish) and MOE (did the operator's end state change) and split REPLAN into "execute branch at trigger" versus "adjust to variance", where the latter uses a RDSP-style one-COA minimal re-plan, because JORDAN currently treats all deviation as one escalation ladder and so pays full replan cost for variances a pre-planned branch could absorb. Kernel (two-line distinction) and eval suite (variance-handling cases).

### 13. Learning: AAR, hot wash, CALL, and why lessons are not learned
**What it is.** The after-action review (AAR) per TC 25-20 asks four questions: what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, why was there a difference, and what should be sustained or improved [recalled]. Ground rules: participation by all ranks, focus on the task not the person, and no blame; **formal** AARs are planned with observers and a facilitator, **informal** (hot wash) are immediate and leader-led. The Center for Army Lessons Learned (CALL) collects and publishes observations and handbooks (the MDMP handbook is an example [verified]). I could not retrieve TC 25-20 or its successor treatment in FM 7-0 (the DTIC copy I identified was blocked by the budget).
**Critiques.** The known pattern is "lessons observed, not learned": an observation becomes a lesson only when someone owns a change to doctrine, training or SOP, and the loop closes only on recurrence testing. The search results include War on the Rocks "Lessons Observed on Lessons Observed" and a CIA study "The Case for After Action Reports" (titles seen, unread). CALL's own existence for an MDMP handbook after CTC trends persisted is the visible example.
**Transfer.** The four AAR questions are a strict schema and are cheap to compute for an agent run (the plan is the "supposed to happen", the transcript is "what happened"). The part JORDAN lacks is *ownership of the change*: a lesson should name the artifact it changes (a kernel line, a hook, a test) and the check that will show the change worked.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should write each AAR as `{expected, actual, cause, change_target, change_check}` where `change_target` must name a file or hook and `change_check` a runnable test, and refuse to archive a lesson without both, because that converts observation into a tracked change and gives the experience store a falsifiable entry. Harness (schema) and eval suite.

### 14. Boyd's OODA, McChrystal's Team of Teams, and Marine maneuver warfare
**What it is.** Boyd's own diagram is not the four-box loop popularized in training slides. Orientation is the center: it is shaped by genetic heritage, cultural traditions, previous experience, new information, and analysis and synthesis, and it feeds decide and act directly through **implicit guidance and control** (trained, shared orientation lets action proceed without explicit orders). The reference reading is Osinga, *Science, Strategy and War* (2007) (listed https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A4211794/view; I did not open it) [source seen, claim recalled]. McChrystal's *Team of Teams* (2015): **shared consciousness** (a daily operations-and-intelligence forum so every team sees the same picture) plus **empowered execution** (small teams act without asking, within a common purpose), supplemented by embedded liaisons [recalled; exact attendance numbers not verified]. MCDP 1 *Warfighting*: friction, uncertainty, fluidity and disorder define the problem; maneuver warfare answers with tempo, **focus of effort**, **surfaces and gaps** (attack where the enemy is weak), and commander's intent with low-level initiative [recalled; the PDF fetch failed]. MCDP 5 *Planning* and MCDP 6 *Command and Control* treat planning as a continuing process of anticipating and adjusting, not producing a fixed plan, and describe decision-making along an intuitive-to-analytical spectrum [recalled]. MCWP 5-10 (Marine Corps Planning Process: problem framing, COA development, wargaming, comparison and decision, orders development, transition) [recalled].
**Critiques.** Boyd's work is a briefing set, not a tested theory; the loop picture misleads because it suggests speed matters more than orientation quality. Team of Teams is a memoir and advocacy book with limited controlled evidence.
**Transfer.** Implicit guidance and control is the strongest idea for agent teams: if workers share an orientation (project conventions, past decisions, the operator's goals), they need fewer instructions. Shared consciousness translates to a shared state file everyone reads, but it has a cost: every shared fact adds to every context. The Marine focus-of-effort idea is JORDAN's main-effort notion and supports the lead naming one decisive worker or subtask.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should keep one small, curated shared-orientation file (conventions, prior rulings, known traps) read by every worker, and measure whether it reduces worker deviations compared with longer briefs, because it tests the implicit-guidance claim directly. Orchestration layer plus eval suite.

### 15. Red teaming and the British Army combat estimate
**What it is.** The UFMCS Red Team Handbook (not retrieved) catalogs structured analytic techniques (key assumptions check, premortem, devil's advocacy, what-if analysis, and others) [recalled]. The British Army combat estimate is a seven-question planning method for tactical commanders (what is the enemy doing and why; what have I been told to do and why; what effects must I have on the enemy; where can I best do each action or effect; what resources do I need; when and where do actions take place relative to each other; what control measures do I need) [recalled; unverified against a current British doctrine source].
**Critiques.** Red teams are often used only after the plan is chosen, or are ignored by a commander who disagrees; I found no controlled evidence.
**Transfer.** The combat estimate is a short, question-based alternative to a 12-phase pipeline, and fits a STANDARD-tier agent more naturally than MDMP.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should test the seven-question combat estimate as the STANDARD-tier planning template against the current PLAN phase on a fixed task set, because it is a smaller artifact with a doctrinal pedigree and the claim is falsifiable. Eval suite.

### 16. Military C2 applied to AI agents, 2024 to 2026
**What it is.** (a) Nichols, "Stop Using AI. Start Commanding It", War on the Rocks, 31 Aug 2026: treat agents as subordinate forces under a human commander. Maps intent (agents must act in intent when cut off), mission-specific qualification instead of general benchmarks, accountability (the commander absorbs it; logs and abort criteria in orders), control (guardrails, kill switches, tool limits), and risk tolerance ("send the model you can afford to lose"); proposes counting standing agents, authority, certification, readiness and spawned subagents [verified]. The author states his evidence is largely a personal hobby setup [verified]. (b) Annett and Giordano (National Defense University), "Synthesized Command and Control (SYNTHComm)", Breaking Defense, 1 May 2026: human approval of individual AI actions comes too late; encode intent as persistent constraints and weighting functions, with low-frequency elements (intent, policy) preserved and high-frequency adaptations amplified or dampened by context; authority as a continuous rather than binary property [verified; the formula is an image]. (c) An unsigned Australian Army "Hello Niner" piece (Cove) on a commander-specific AI agent: warns of cognitive debt, staff displacement and authority drift to the AI [verified; no author or date given]. (d) Titles seen in search but not read, so content unverified: arXiv 2608.20597 "Testing and Evaluation of Agentic AI Systems In Military Command and Control"; arXiv 2510.02557 "Orchestrating Human-AI Teams: The Manager Agent as a ..."; Scale AI "Agentic Warfare: Evolving Doctrine"; US Army War College "From Reliability to Fit: Trust, Adaptation and Distributed Cognition in AI-enabled..."; Air University "Mission Command".
**Critiques.** All verified items are opinion or concept pieces, not experiments. The Cove piece and Nichols both identify the risk that decision authority drifts to the machine.
**Transfer.** What is new relative to JORDAN: authority as a continuously governed property (SYNTHComm), certification before delegation (Nichols), and audit logs and abort criteria written into the order. What does not transfer: lethal-use and contested-communications concerns that dominate these papers; and nobody here provides a measurement.
**V3 implication candidate.** V3 should record, per worker dispatch, a delegation record (model, task class, authority granted, tools allowed, abort criteria) and aggregate it, so that "competence" can be earned from a track record rather than asserted. Harness (dispatch log) and eval suite.

## Contradictions with JORDAN v2

- **BACKBRIEF placement and meaning.** JORDAN puts BACKBRIEF as a planner's DAG check right after PLAN. Doctrine's backbrief and confirmation brief are subordinate-to-commander understanding checks after the order is issued. JORDAN's phase is useful, but its name misleads the design team and omits the real check.
- **Parallelism.** Doctrine's core planning idea is parallel, overlapping work via warning orders. JORDAN's pipeline is serial across 12 phases.
- **Tier ceremony versus doctrine's scaling.** Doctrine scales planning by method: TLP for small units, abbreviated MDMP when time is short, RDSP during execution, ADM for ill-structured problems. JORDAN scales by tier but uses one pipeline shape.
- **Approval on initial risk.** Doctrine approves on *residual* risk and delegates acceptance authority by level; JORDAN fuses pre-control risk with premortem severity.
- **Intent definition.** JORDAN's "Goal + Constraints + Acceptance Criteria + Priority" is not commander's intent. Intent has purpose (why), key tasks that survive plan failure, and end state; acceptance criteria are closer to measures of effectiveness. Constraints are a mission-analysis output, not part of intent.
- **FRAGO.** JORDAN uses "FRAGO replan" as a cheaper replan. Doctrinally a FRAGO is an order that carries a decision already made (a branch, sequel or modification); the deciding is RDSP or MDMP. JORDAN conflates the product with the process.
- **One verification channel versus two decision types.** JORDAN's EVALUATE has no MOP/MOE split.

## Hype and weak evidence

- Boyd's "faster OODA wins" slide-deck version is a misreading of Boyd's own model (orientation is central); evidence for a simple speed claim is weak.
- *Team of Teams* is advocacy and memoir; no controlled evidence found.
- "Mission command works" is asserted by doctrine; the critiques (NDU 2020, MWI) argue armies fail to implement it, which means doctrine is not evidence that it works as practiced.
- The one-third/two-thirds rule and the one-fifth/four-fifths shift are rules of thumb; I found no empirical test.
- 2026 AI-and-C2 articles (Nichols, Annett and Giordano, Cove) are opinion or concept pieces with no experiments.
- The decision matrix gives numbers but is subjective; do not import it as scoring without the critiques.
